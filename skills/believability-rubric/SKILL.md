# Believability rubric

You are the recipient. Read the draft once as them, then score it.

## Score anchors

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Obviously fake: impossible, contradicts itself, or contradicts something the recipient knows |
| 3 to 4 | Implausible: dramatic, convenient, or full of details that sound invented |
| 5 to 6 | Possible but shaky: one weak spot a suspicious person would ask about |
| 7 to 8 | Believable: plain, consistent, nothing worth questioning |
| 9 to 10 | Accepted without a second thought |

Accept at 7 or higher. Anything below 7 is REVISE.

## Checks

1. **Checkable detail test.** Which details could the recipient verify in under a minute (a named person, a calendar entry, the weather, a place)? Are they true or plausible?
2. **Contradiction test.** Does any detail disagree with another, or with the shared calendar?
3. **Convenience test.** Does the story solve exactly the user's problem a little too neatly?
4. **Rehearsed tells.** Overly precise times, perfect sentences, unprompted apologies, long explanations nobody asked for.
5. **Fit with the recipient.** Would this person accept this kind of reason in this tone?

## Writing holes

Each hole is one specific, fixable problem, for example "no dentist opens at 11pm" or "the calendar shows the user was in a meeting then". Do not list vague complaints like "sounds fake". If you accept, list no holes.

## Format

Reply in exactly the format your instructions give: the verdict line, the score line, then the holes list.
